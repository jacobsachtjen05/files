# My Narrative Responses

I think of narrative as any experience that structures meaning through sequence, character, and stakes. Stories do not only live in books or movies. They also live in games, podcasts, and interactive worlds. Three narratives I know especially well are Critical Role, Skylanders Spyros Adventure, and Baldur’s Gate 3. Each one works differently, but all of them create emotional engagement in a way that feels personal to me.

## 1 **Critical Role**

### Why is it interesting to you?

Critical Role is interesting to me because it is emotionally rich, visually imaginative, and built from the energy of live performance. It is not just a fantasy story in the abstract. It is a story shaped by voice, chemistry, and the way a group of performers builds a world together. I love the way the cast makes even small moments feel meaningful, whether it is a joke, a tense decision, or a quiet emotional beat. I love the way the players make deliberate choices. They are not just performing lines. They are reacting to one another and building the story in real time.

### What is the data in this narrative?

The data here is not only the plot, but also the sound, timing, performance, and social dynamics between the cast members. The narrative is built from voice acting, pauses, reactions, jokes, and the decisions that emerge from the live table experience. There is also a huge amount of data about world building, maps, lore, monster design, character arcs, and the relationship between each player’s choices and the larger story. In a way, the story is made from social and performative information as much as it is made from plot. The emotional meaning comes from how the performers respond to each other and to the world they are creating.

### How does the form impact your experience?

The audio visual livestream format is a huge part of what makes Critical Role feel so alive. I hear the players and see the world come together in real time, which creates a strong sense of immersion. Because it is live and collaborative, it feels less like a polished final product and more like being in the room while the story is being made. That gives the experience a different emotional texture than a traditional book or movie. I think it would feel weaker as a simple audio format because the visual performance and player chemistry are such a big part of the experience. The live, collaborative structure is essential to the narrative’s energy.

### If the original creator were to retell the narrative today, how would you want them to do it?

I would want them to keep the live, collaborative format, but add more accessible ways to revisit key story moments. For example, I would love chapter markers, character arc summaries, and deeper digital archives for lore and world building. I think that would make the story easier to navigate for new audiences without losing the magic of the original performance. At the same time, I would want the retelling to preserve the emotional unpredictability that comes from live storytelling. The ideal modern version would still feel like a real shared experience, but with better tools for discovery and memory.

## 2 **Skylanders Spyros Adventure**

### Why is it interesting to you?

Skylanders Spyros Adventure is interesting to me because it mixes action, toy collecting, and fantasy world building in a way that feels playful but still narratively meaningful. I grew up with the game, and the story was important to me because it made the world feel magical and full of personality. The whole premise of using magical Skylanders to protect the world from evil creates a straightforward but exciting adventure structure. Even though it is not as emotionally heavy as some of the other stories I like, it is still memorable because it connects imagination, characters, and a sense of discovery. Finally, the allure of being able to save the Skylanders by returning them to their world created a sense of urgency. I wanted to obtain these figures and play the game with them.

### What is the data in this narrative?

The data in Skylanders includes the level design, collectible figures, unlockable abilities, boss encounters, and the progression of the overall story. The narrative is not only in the cutscenes. It is also in the way the game teaches you to move through the world, collect items, and use each character’s powers. The toy figures themselves act like story objects because they represent different characters and abilities, and the player’s collection becomes part of the experience. In other words, the narrative data is a mix of environment, object collection, and character identity. The world tells a story through action, accumulation, and progression.

### How does the form impact your experience?

The game form matters a lot here because the world is designed to be played, not just watched. The physical toys and the console interface create a hybrid narrative experience where the story crosses from digital play into physical collection. That made the game feel more personal to me because I was not just playing a story, I was building an imagined world through the objects I owned and the characters I chose. If it were told as a movie or a book, I think it would lose some of the fun and excitement that comes from collecting and using different Skylanders in battle. The toy based action platformer form is a big part of what makes the narrative memorable.

### If the original creator were to retell the narrative today, how would you want them to do it?

I would want them to keep the toy and gameplay connection but make the story more complex and connected to the world. A modern retelling could include deeper character arcs, more dynamic quests, and more ways for players to build relationships between characters. I think it would also be cool if the game used the toys in a more meaningful way, not just as collectibles but as pieces of the story itself. The strongest version would still be a game, because the interactive, collectible, and exploratory parts are what make it unique. I would want it to keep the sense of wonder but make the narrative feel a little more layered and emotionally rich.

## 3 **Baldur’s Gate 3**

### Why is it interesting to you

Baldur’s Gate 3 is interesting to me because it turns player choice into a form of narrative, and because I have such a personal relationship to how it unfolds. On my first playthrough, I got completely lost in all the side content. I kept following quests, exploring random locations, and getting pulled into side stories that were fun, interesting, and surprisingly emotional, but before I knew it, I had lost track of the main story. I remember feeling confused and a little overwhelmed because the game was so full of possibilities. Eventually I had to step back, orient myself, and figure out how to get back to the central plot. That experience made the game feel even more meaningful to me because it taught me how narrative in a game can be both enormous and personal at the same time.

### What is the data in this narrative

The data in Baldur’s Gate 3 includes dialogue branches, character relationships, party dynamics, outcomes based on statistics, and the evolving state of the world. For me, it also includes the experience of getting lost and then finding my way back. The narrative is built from a combination of written text, voice acting, animation, and decision making by the player. In a sense, the story is a database of choices. Each decision creates a different chain of consequences, and each chosen response changes the meaning of future scenes. The data is not only plot information. It is the structure of possibility itself. The narrative emerges from a web of relationships and decisions rather than a single fixed path.

### How does the form impact your experience

The RPG form is essential to what makes this story compelling. A film or a book could tell me the main arc, but it could not let me inhabit the story in the same way. I think the game works so well because it lets me experience narrative as an active process, not just a passive sequence of events. On my first run, getting lost inside content made the story feel more alive, because I was discovering the world organically instead of following a strict path. That said, it also made me realize how easy it is for a huge narrative world to overwhelm the player. If it were told in a more linear form, the sense of freedom would be reduced, but the clarity might improve. The interactive form is what gives the story its intensity and its messiness, and I think that is part of why it stays with me.

### If the original creator were to retell the narrative today, how would you want them to do it

Honestly, the appeal of the game is the massive amount of content inside the experience. If I were to change a single thing about the narrative, it would take away from the appeal. The story and the narrative are what you make them out to be, and they are unique to every player. For this example, I do not think much should be changed. If I had to change anything about the story, I would develop two companion characters further, specifically Karlach and Wyll. They were added late in development, and they have less character development than the rest of the cast.

## Reflection

Across all three narratives, I notice that the form changes the emotional rhythm of the story. In a podcast like Critical Role, the story is built from voice, timing, and live reaction. In a game like Skylanders Spyros Adventure, it is built through play, collection, and fantasy action. In Baldur’s Gate 3, it is built through choice and relationship. Narrative is not just what happens. It is also how the medium asks us to participate. That is why I am interested in narrative in digital contexts. The interface is not just a container for the story, but part of the story’s meaning.
